About

Why this site exists

Everything here is written by one person, tested in one small flat, and cited so you can check it. Including the parts where the honest answer is “nobody really knows yet.”

I spent about six years with a gym membership I used, generously, twice a month. Not because I did not care — because the gym was a twenty-minute walk away, closed by the time I got a child to sleep, and required a bag I never packed. The problem was never motivation. It was that the plan assumed a life I did not have.

What eventually worked was embarrassingly small: about six square feet of floor between a sofa and a radiator, twenty minutes, three times a week, and a stubborn refusal to skip two sessions in a row. That is the whole method. This site is what I learned making it stick, and what I found when I went looking for the evidence behind the advice everyone repeats.

What I am, and what I am not

I am not a doctor. I am not a physiotherapist. I do not hold a strength and conditioning certification, and I am not going to imply one by wearing a lab coat in a stock photo. If a post here touches on anything clinical — pain, injury, pregnancy, medication, a diagnosed condition — the correct move is to talk to someone qualified, and I will say so in the post rather than bury it in a footer.

What I do have is several years of training in a very small space, a reasonable tolerance for reading research papers, and no financial reason to tell you that you need to buy anything. That combination turns out to be rarer in this niche than it should be.

The editorial rules I hold myself to
  • Every statistic or health claim gets a real, linked source. I have never cited a study I have not opened.
  • No absolute claims. “Some evidence suggests” and “may help with” are not hedging for its own sake — they are usually the accurate description.
  • Where the research is thin, contested or mostly done on twenty-year-old male undergraduates, the post says so.
  • Every post carries a visible last-updated date. If it has gone stale, you can see that.
  • If I recommend a piece of equipment, I tell you when the cheap version is fine and when you can skip it entirely.

Who this is for

People with a full-time job, a small flat, and somewhere between fifteen and thirty minutes. People training above someone else's ceiling. Parents whose sessions get interrupted. Anyone who has downloaded a twelve-week programme, managed nine days of it, and concluded the problem was them.

It is not for people training for a powerlifting meet, and it is not a weight-loss site. There is good writing on both of those elsewhere.

How the site is organised

Five topics, each built around one comprehensive guide with more specific posts branching off it:

  • Bodyweight Strength — the movements themselves, and how to progress them.
  • Quick Workouts & Plans — sessions and schedules that fit real weeks.
  • Small-Space Training — thin walls, low ceilings, no room.
  • Minimal Gear — what to buy, in what order, and what to ignore.
  • Habits & Recovery — sleep, soreness, food, and why week six is where people quit.

If you are starting from nothing, the 4-week beginner plan is the front door. If you have twenty minutes right now, try the 15-minute full-body workout.

How this site makes money

Display advertising, and eventually affiliate links on equipment posts. Two commitments about that: an affiliate link never changes which product I recommend, and if the honest answer is “you do not need this,” the post says that even though it earns nothing. Ad placements are marked and kept out of the middle of workout instructions.

Corrections

If I have got something wrong — a misread study, a broken link, a form cue that does not hold up — tell me. Corrections get made in the post itself and the updated date changes. I would rather be corrected than consistent.
