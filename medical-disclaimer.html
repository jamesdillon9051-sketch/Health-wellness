The short version

This website is general information, not medical advice. It cannot examine you, does not know your history, and is not a substitute for a qualified professional. Talk to your doctor before starting any new exercise programme.

Reading this website, subscribing to its email list or contacting its author does not create a doctor–patient, therapist–client, coach–athlete or any other professional relationship. The author of this site is not a physician, physiotherapist, registered dietitian or certified strength and conditioning specialist, and does not hold any clinical qualification. Content reflects personal experience and published research, not clinical expertise.

Consult a qualified professional first

Always seek the advice of your physician or another suitably qualified health provider before beginning any exercise programme, changing your diet, or acting on anything you read here. This is particularly important if any of the following apply to you:

  • You are pregnant, have recently given birth, or are trying to conceive.
  • You have a diagnosed cardiovascular condition, high or low blood pressure, or a heart rhythm disorder.
  • You are recovering from surgery, an injury, or an acute illness.
  • You have a musculoskeletal condition, chronic pain, or a history of joint or spinal problems.
  • You live with diabetes, a metabolic condition, an eating disorder, or any chronic illness.
  • You take prescription medication that affects heart rate, blood pressure, balance or blood sugar.
  • You are over 65, or have been sedentary for an extended period.
  • You are unsure, for any reason, whether exercise is safe for you right now.

Never delay seeking medical advice

Never disregard professional medical advice, and never delay seeking it, because of something you have read on this website. If you think you may be experiencing a medical emergency, contact your local emergency services immediately.

Stop if something is wrong

Exercise carries inherent risk. Stop immediately and seek medical attention if you experience chest pain or tightness, shortness of breath disproportionate to the effort, dizziness or fainting, an irregular heartbeat, sharp or sudden joint pain, numbness or tingling, or pain that persists after the session ends. Ordinary muscular fatigue and delayed-onset soreness are expected; the symptoms above are not.

You voluntarily choose to participate in any activity described on this site, and you do so at your own risk. Exercise instructions here are written for a general audience and cannot account for your individual health status, injury history, fitness level, environment or equipment. It is your responsibility to judge whether a given exercise is appropriate for you, to use safe equipment and a safe space, and to stop when something does not feel right.
